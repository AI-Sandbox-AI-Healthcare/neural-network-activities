To learn that an early input matters, the gradient of a late loss must travel back through every step in between (back-propagation through time). Each step multiplies it by a factor — and many factors multiplied together quickly vanish or explode. You will measure those factors in a one-unit RNN.

Q1 What factor does each step back multiply the gradient by?
Q2 Why can't we just tune the weight to keep that factor at 1?
Q3 Which fixes work for vanishing gradients, and which for exploding ones?

Why these questions: they explain why RNNs struggle with long dependencies, and what is done about it.

  • Q1 → Stage 1 and the first question of Stage 2: measure how much of the gradient tanh lets through, then combine it with w.
  • Q2 → Stage 2: back-propagate through ten steps and try to tune w and u.
  • Q3 → Stage 3: match each fix to the problem it solves.

Stage 3 — Choosing the Right Fix
Takeaway
  • Each step back multiplies the gradient by w·tanh′ (for vector states, by Wᵀ·diag(1 − h²)). Over many steps the product shrinks (vanishes) or grows (explodes) exponentially.
  • tanh′ collapses once |pre-activation| ≳ 1.8, so saturated units block the gradient.
  • You cannot simply tune w: with no input the factor is w per step, so w < 1 vanishes, w > 1 explodes, and only w ≈ 1 holds steady. With real inputs a large w saturates h and the factor collapses.
  • Exploding → clip the gradient norm. Vanishing → gated units (LSTM/GRU) whose self-loop can stay near 1. ReLU does not remove the problem.
