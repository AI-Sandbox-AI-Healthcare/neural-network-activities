Build an RNN that reads one word and writes its dictionary definition — a word sequence of any length. Make five design decisions, then run a small trained decoder to see why they matter.

Q1 How does the input word get into the network?
Q2 How does each step know what has been written so far?
Q3 How does the network produce words — and stop?

Why these questions: they are the design decisions behind any RNN that turns one input into a sequence.

  • Q1 → Decisions 1–2: how the input word is represented and where it enters.
  • Q2 → Decision 3 and Stage 6: what each step feeds back in, and what goes wrong without it.
  • Q3 → Decisions 4–5 and Stage 6: choose the output and stopping rule, then decode word by word until <END>.

Takeaway
  • Input: a one-hot word times a learned matrix is an embedding lookup; feeding it (R·x) at every step keeps it fresh however long the output grows.
  • Feedback: h⁽ᵗ⁾ = tanh(b + W·h⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾ + U·y⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾ + R·x) — without the previous word y⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾, later steps cannot adapt to what was actually written.
  • Output and stopping: ŷ⁽ᵗ⁾ = softmax(V·h⁽ᵗ⁾ + c) over the vocabulary, with an <END> token that ends the sequence.
  • Training vs. generating: training feeds back the true previous word (teacher forcing); generation feeds back the model's own choices.
