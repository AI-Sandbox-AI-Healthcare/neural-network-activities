A 1-D text CNN slides a window of k consecutive words over a sentence, extracts every n-gram, scores each with a learned filter, then max-pools. Explore what k is needed to capture short-range context — and what breaks for long-range negation.

Stage 2 — The Bigram Insight

Sentence A contains "good" yet is negative; Sentence B contains "bad" yet is positive. A k=1 filter can't separate them. Both end with either "being bad" or "being good" — context is the critical bigram.

What is the minimum k that captures "being bad" / "being good" inside one n-gram?
Stage 3 — The Negation Gap
It[1]   wasn't[2]   the[3]   case[4]   that[5]   he[6]   was[7]   unhappy[8]

"wasn't" is at position 2, "unhappy" is at position 8. Both must appear inside the same n-gram for the filter to see their relationship.

What is the minimum k so that both words sit inside one window?
Stage 4 — CNN Limitation

A k=2 filter trained on A & B learns "being bad"→negative and "being good"→positive. Applied to Sentence C, the highest-scoring bigram is "was unhappy" — strongly negative. The negation "wasn't" seven words earlier is invisible.
