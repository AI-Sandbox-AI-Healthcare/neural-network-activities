Strip an RNN down to a single number. The state h is updated from the previous state and the new input, with no activation function. Two weights are involved: w carries the old state forward and u brings in the new input. Which one decides how long the past is remembered?

Takeaway
  • Unrolled: h⁽ᵀ⁾ = Σk u·wT−k·x⁽ᵏ⁾. An input ℓ steps old carries the weight u·wℓ, which is also the gradient ∂h⁽ᵀ⁾/∂x⁽ᵀ⁻ℓ⁾.
  • w controls memory; u only scales. With |w| < 1 the past fades (at w = 0.9, below a tenth after 22 steps), with |w| = 1 it is kept, and with |w| > 1 it explodes. A negative w only alternates the sign. u cancels in every ratio.
  • Constant input: h settles at u / (1 − w) only when |w| < 1. Otherwise it grows without bound, however small u is.
  • Many units: the eigenvalues of W play the role of w (Goodfellow §10.7). Each eigen-direction is scaled by λt; entries below 1 do not guarantee stability.
  • Next (10.10): an LSTM's forget gate is a w that the network sets at every step, close to 1 while something must be remembered.
